The warm papers
Same lightness and near-identical chroma across all five, so only the hue changes — that's what makes them swappable without re-tuning anything else. Ink stays #2E2A24 throughout.
Butter — yellow. The warmest and the most "lamp on in the evening". Careful: it's the closest to the amber reveal colour, so the winner's glow loses some of its exclusivity. Would want the reveal accent pushed to rust if you pick this.
Clay — orange. Terracotta, unglazed. Sits under the rust accent beautifully and makes the forest green look deliberate rather than default. The most personal of the warm set, and my pick if you stay warm. Live now.
Blush — red. Dusty rose, softest of the five. Genuinely differentiating because almost nothing in this category goes pink, but it puts a strong personality on every screen — worth living with for a day before committing.
Fawn — brown, hue pulled almost to zero. The quietest option: reads as "unbleached" rather than as a colour. The one to choose if you want the jars and slips to be the only thing anyone notices.
The greens — Sage reads paper-first, Olive sits closest to kraft, Eucalyptus commits (and needs the primary button darkened to #2F4839), Celadon is coolest and stops reading organic altogether. The reason they differentiate isn't the green: it's brown ink on a cool ground, which is a pairing almost nothing in this category does.
Keeping the two registers related
The dark screens no longer have their own colours. Every surface and text tone on title detail and rating entry is now mixed from the active paper — so Clay gives you a warm brown-black, Celadon a cool green-black, Blush a soft red-black. Switch papers and watch the dark screens follow. Same relationship, different hue; nothing to re-pick when you change your mind about the background.
Amber (#C98A3C) is the one colour that stays fixed across both registers — winner reveal, and now the rating ticks. It's the app's single "this is the good part" signal, so it shouldn't drift with the paper.
New: rating entry. Ten ticks per category, not a slider — tap to set, each number a detent. Reached from title detail via "Mark a card". Try setting Soundtrack; the average recomputes.
The jars
The sleek versions all do the same structural thing: get the label off the glass. A taped-on paper label is what makes a jar read craft-fair; the moment the name sits below or is etched into the surface, the object turns into product design and the warmth has to come from the palette and type instead. That's a better division of labour anyway.
Apothecary — clear glass, thin dark rim, one hard specular highlight, name set beneath in the serif. The most realistic and the most personal: the slips inside become the colour, so a full jar and an empty one look genuinely different. My recommendation.
Pressed — ribbed glass with the name etched straight on in tracked caps. Closest to a real preserving jar, most texture, hardest to read at small sizes.
Line-drawn — your pick, and live now. 1.5px outline, no fill except the tinted contents, name sitting inside the empty upper half. It ties straight back to the line-art storefront in your first reference, and it's the only style that would survive being replaced by a hand-drawn SVG later: swap the outline for your own drawing and everything else — fill level, label position, tap target — still works. If you draw your own, keep the stroke at 1.5px and leave the top 45% clear for the name.
Ceramic — opaque, matte, flat lid. Clean and calm, but you lose the fill level entirely, which is the one piece of information the jar shape was earning you.
Worth deciding
1. If you go green, the slips stay cream — that contrast is doing a lot of work. Should the drawn slips in the pick flow stay cream too, or go white to feel freshly pulled?
2. Apothecary and Line-drawn both push the app cooler. Worth re-testing the Caveat handwriting against them — it may need to drop to only the winner reveal.
3. Tabs vs swipe is still open. If you want, I can build the tab version on the same palette so you're comparing like for like.
